Domain 0 — Core Knowledge

Numbers: mixed, rounded, prime, standard form

A number can take many different forms — a fraction, a mixed number, a decimal, or standard index form. These forms look different but can represent the exact same quantity.

01Mixed Numbers Essential

Before looking at mixed numbers, let us recall two words: in a fraction, the top number is the numerator and the bottom number is the denominator. A proper fraction has a numerator smaller than the denominator (e.g. ½). An improper fraction (or top-heavy fraction) has a numerator greater than or equal to the denominator (e.g. 7⁄4, 7⁄7).

A mixed number is a whole number followed by a proper fraction — for example, four and a third.

📖 Definitions
Numerator
The top number of a fraction. numérateur
Denominator
The bottom number of a fraction. dénominateur
Proper fraction
A fraction where the numerator is smaller than the denominator. fraction propre
Improper / top-heavy fraction
A fraction where the numerator is greater than or equal to the denominator. fraction impropre
Mixed number
A whole number followed by a proper fraction. nombre mixte

Converting an improper fraction to a mixed number

Take 7⁄2. Divide 7 by 2: the quotient is 3 and the remainder is 1. So 7⁄2 = 3½ — the quotient becomes the whole number and the remainder is placed over the divisor.

\(\dfrac{7}{2} \;\longrightarrow\; 3 \text{ R } 1 \;\longrightarrow\; 3\tfrac{1}{2}\)

Going backwards

To convert 3½ back to an improper fraction: 3 × 2 = 6, plus 1 = 7, so 3½ = 7⁄2.

📐 Why the value does not change

When we write 7⁄2 = 3½, we use the fact that 7 = 3×2 + 1. So \(\frac{7}{2} = \frac{3\times2+1}{2} = \frac{3\times2}{2} + \frac{1}{2} = 3 + \frac12 = 3\tfrac12\). The mixed number is just a different notation for the same rational number — no approximation is involved.

✏️ Worked example

Convert 38⁄7 into a mixed number, then express it as a decimal rounded to two decimal places.

1. Mixed number: 38 ÷ 7 = 5 R 3 (since 7 × 5 = 35 and 38 − 35 = 3). So 38⁄7 = 5 3⁄7.

2. Decimal: 3⁄7 ≈ 0.428571…, so 38⁄7 ≈ 5.428571…

3. Rounding to 2 d.p.: keep 5.42, look at the third digit (8 ≥ 5), so round up: 5.43.

Final answer: 38⁄7 ≈ 5.43 (to 2 d.p.). Note: 5 3⁄7 is exact; 5.43 is only an approximation.

⚠ Common mistake

Writing “5.3” for 5 3⁄7 — the mixed number 5 3⁄7 means 5 + 3⁄7, not 5.3. Always keep the fraction bar visible in the mixed form.

🧩 Summary
  • A mixed number is an exact alternative notation for an improper fraction.
  • Quotient → whole number, remainder → numerator over the divisor.
  • Rounding changes the value; converting to a mixed number does not.

02Rounding Numbers Essential

Rounding is useful when a decimal has many digits and we want a simpler, more readable value. There are two common ways: rounding to a number of decimal places (d.p.), or to a number of significant figures (s.f.).

📖 Definitions
Decimal place
A position after the decimal point. décimale
Significant figure
A digit that carries meaning in a number, starting from the first non-zero digit. chiffre significatif
Leading zero
A zero at the start of a number that is not significant. zéro non significatif

Rounding to decimal places

Take 3.14159 and round it to 2 d.p. We keep the first two digits after the point (1 and 4), then look at the next digit (1). Since 1 < 5, we do not change anything: 3.14159 → 3.14.

For 3.146 to 2 d.p.: the third digit is 6 ≥ 5, so we round up: 3.146 → 3.15.

Rounding to significant figures

Round 0.0523 to 2 s.f. The first significant figure is 5, the second is 2 — the leading zeros do not count. The next digit is 3 < 5, so the second figure stays the same: 0.0523 → 0.052.

Rule: keep the digits you need, look at the next one, and round up if it is 5 or more.
✏️ Worked example

Round 3.14159 to 2 d.p. and 0.0523 to 2 s.f.

3.14159 → 3.14 (next digit 1 < 5).

0.0523 → 0.052 (next digit 3 < 5, leading zeros ignored).

🧩 Summary
  • Rounding replaces an exact value by an approximation — always state the precision (e.g. “to 2 d.p.”).
  • For significant figures, ignore leading zeros; they are not significant.

03Prime Numbers Essential

A prime number is a number bigger than 1 that can only be divided by 1 and itself. For example: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, …

7 is prime: its only divisors are 1 and 7. 12 is not prime — it can be divided by 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, and 12 — we call it a composite number.

📖 Definitions
Prime number
An integer greater than 1 whose only positive divisors are 1 and itself. nombre premier
Composite number
An integer greater than 1 that is not prime. nombre composé
Prime factorization
Writing a number as a product of prime numbers. décomposition en facteurs premiers

Prime factorization

Every composite number can be broken down into a product of prime numbers, and this factorization is unique (up to order). For example: 12 = 2² × 3.

Every integer \(n \ge 2\) has a unique prime factorization.
📐 Proof — there are infinitely many prime numbers (Euclid)

Suppose, for contradiction, that there are only finitely many primes \(p_1, p_2, \dots, p_n\). Consider \(N = p_1 \times p_2 \times \cdots \times p_n + 1\). None of the \(p_i\) divides \(N\) (dividing \(N\) by any \(p_i\) leaves remainder 1). But every integer ≥ 2 has at least one prime divisor, so \(N\) must have a prime divisor not among the \(p_i\) — contradiction. Hence there are infinitely many primes.

✏️ Worked example

Let \(N\) be the product of the first five prime numbers. Calculate \(N\) and express it in standard index form.

1. First five primes: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11.

2. \(N = 2 \times 3 \times 5 \times 7 \times 11 = 2310\).

3. Standard form: \(2310 = 2.31 \times 10^3\). Check: \(2.31 \times 1000 = 2310\) ✓.

🧩 Summary
  • Primes are the “building blocks” of whole numbers.
  • To test whether \(n\) is prime, it is enough to look for divisors up to \(\sqrt{n}\).
  • The factorization is always unique — this is the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic.

04Standard Index Form Essential

Standard index form (also called scientific notation) is useful for very large or very small numbers. A number in standard form looks like \(a \times 10^n\), where \(1 \le a < 10\) and \(n\) is an integer.

\(a \times 10^n\)   with   \(1 \le a < 10, \; n \in \mathbb{Z}\)

Large numbers

5,600,000: move the decimal point 6 places to the left → 5.6 × 10⁶.

Small numbers

0.00043: move the decimal point 4 places to the right → 4.3 × 10⁻⁴. The negative exponent tells us the number is smaller than 1.

💡 Universal notation

Standard index form is used worldwide — in British and American textbooks, and in international science journals — which shows that mathematics is a truly universal language.

✏️ Worked example

Express 2310 in standard index form.

1. Coefficient between 1 and 10: 2.31.

2. Moved the point 3 places to the left → multiply by 10³.

3. \(2310 = 2.31 \times 10^3\).

🧩 Summary
  • Standard form is \(a \times 10^n\) with \(1 \le a < 10\).
  • Large numbers have positive exponents; small numbers have negative exponents.

05Ratios Recommended

A ratio compares two or more quantities of the same kind. It can be written as \(a : b\), as a fraction \(\frac{a}{b}\), or in words (“a to b”).

📖 Definitions
Ratio
A comparison of two or more quantities. rapport / ratio
Proportion
An equation stating that two ratios are equal. proportion
Scale factor
The number by which all parts of a ratio are multiplied. coefficient de proportionnalité
If \(a : b = c : d\), then \(ad = bc\) (cross-multiplication).
✏️ Worked example

Divide £120 in the ratio 2 : 3.

1. Total parts = 2 + 3 = 5.

2. One part = 120 ÷ 5 = £24.

3. Shares: 2 × 24 = £48 and 3 × 24 = £72.

Check: 48 + 72 = 120 ✓ and 48 : 72 = 2 : 3 ✓.

🧩 Summary
  • To share in a ratio: add the parts, divide the total, multiply each share.
  • Always check that the shares add back to the original total.

✏️ Worked example

A triangle has sides 6.2 cm, 8.6 cm and 4 cm, and a height of 4 cm relative to the base of 8.6 cm. Find its perimeter and area.

1. Perimeter: \(P = 6.2 + 8.6 + 4 = 18.8\) cm.

2. Area: \(A = \dfrac{8.6 \times 4}{2} = \dfrac{34.4}{2} = 17.2\) cm².

🧩 Summary
  • Triangles are classified by sides (equilateral / isosceles / scalene) and by angles (right / acute / obtuse).
  • Perimeter = sum of all sides; Area = (base × height) ÷ 2.
  • The angle sum of a triangle is always 180°.

07Pythagoras’ Theorem & Trigonometric Ratios Essential

In a right-angled triangle, the side opposite the right angle is called the hypotenuse — it is always the longest side. Pythagoras’ theorem relates the three sides of any right-angled triangle.

Pythagoras: \(\;a^2 + b^2 = c^2\;\) where \(c\) is the hypotenuse.
📖 Definitions
Hypotenuse
The side opposite the right angle; the longest side. hypoténuse
Adjacent
The side next to a given angle. côté adjacent
Opposite
The side opposite a given angle. côté opposé
📐 Proof — a visual proof of Pythagoras’ theorem

Consider a square of side \(a+b\) containing four copies of a right-angled triangle with legs \(a\) and \(b\) and hypotenuse \(c\), arranged so that the remaining space forms a tilted square of side \(c\). The total area of the large square is \((a+b)^2 = a^2+2ab+b^2\). The area of the four triangles is \(4 \times \frac12 ab = 2ab\), so the area of the tilted square is \((a+b)^2 - 2ab = a^2+b^2\). But the tilted square has area \(c^2\). Hence \(a^2+b^2=c^2\).

Trigonometric ratios (SOH CAH TOA)

\(\sin\theta = \dfrac{\text{Opp}}{\text{Hyp}} \qquad \cos\theta = \dfrac{\text{Adj}}{\text{Hyp}} \qquad \tan\theta = \dfrac{\text{Opp}}{\text{Adj}}\)
📖 Legend
Opp
Side opposite the angle. côté opposé
Adj
Side next to the angle. côté adjacent
Hyp
Hypotenuse (the longest side). hypoténuse
✏️ Worked example

In a right-angled triangle, the two shorter sides measure 5 cm and 12 cm. Find the hypotenuse and the smallest angle.

1. Pythagoras: \(c^2 = 5^2 + 12^2 = 25 + 144 = 169\), so \(c = \sqrt{169} = 13\) cm.

2. Smallest angle: the smallest angle is opposite the smallest side (5 cm). \(\tan\theta = \frac{5}{12}\), so \(\theta = \tan^{-1}(5/12) \approx 22.6°\).

3. Check: \(13^2 = 169 = 5^2+12^2\) ✓. This is a 5-12-13 triangle, a classic Pythagorean triple.

⚠ Common mistake

Forgetting to check which side is the hypotenuse before applying Pythagoras. The hypotenuse is always opposite the right angle, never one of the two sides that form the right angle.

🧩 Summary
  • Pythagoras: \(a^2 + b^2 = c^2\) where \(c\) is the hypotenuse.
  • SOH CAH TOA: sin = Opp/Hyp, cos = Adj/Hyp, tan = Opp/Adj.
  • Use inverse trig functions (\(\sin^{-1}\), \(\cos^{-1}\), \(\tan^{-1}\)) to find an unknown angle.

07.1Full worked examples — complete oral presentation Recommended

This section reproduces a complete oral presentation (Mizgin & Success, TG2, ~14 min) combining Pythagoras’ theorem and trigonometric ratios through three progressive exercises.

ℹ️ Key vocabulary

Hypotenuse — the side opposite the right angle (the longest). Opposite — the side opposite the angle we are looking at. Adjacent — the side next to the angle. Sine = Opp / Hyp. Tangent = Opp / Adj.

Exercise 1 — Shared side, Pythagoras applied twice

Two right-angled triangles ABC and ACD share the side AC. We know that AB = 20 cm, BC = 12 cm and CD = 9 cm. Calculate the length AD, rounded to three significant figures.

✏️ Step 1 — Find AC in triangle ABC

AB is the hypotenuse (opposite the right angle). Apply Pythagoras:

\(AC^2 + 12^2 = 20^2\)

\(AC^2 = 400 - 144 = 256\)

\(AC = \sqrt{256} = 16 \text{ cm}\)

✏️ Step 2 — Find AD in triangle ACD

Now AC = 16 cm and CD = 9 cm. AD is the hypotenuse:

\(AD^2 = AC^2 + CD^2 = 16^2 + 9^2 = 256 + 81 = 337\)

\(AD = \sqrt{337} \approx 18.3576 \text{ cm}\)

Rounded to 3 s.f.: AD ≈ 18.4 cm.

💡 Key point

The two triangles share AC. We first find AC exactly (16 cm), then use it to find AD. Never round the shared side too early — otherwise the error propagates into the second calculation.

Exercise 2 — Ladder against a wall (unit conversion)

A ladder reaches 2.4 m up a vertical wall. The base of the ladder is 70 cm from the base of the wall on horizontal ground. Find the length of the ladder.

⚠ Watch the units

The height is in metres (2.4 m), the horizontal distance in centimetres (70 cm). Convert first: 2.4 m = 240 cm.

✏️ Solution

The wall is vertical, the ground horizontal, the ladder is the hypotenuse. Apply Pythagoras:

\(240^2 + 70^2 = L^2\)

\(L^2 = 57\,600 + 4\,900 = 62\,500\)

\(L = \sqrt{62\,500} = 250 \text{ cm}\)

Conversion: 250 cm = 2.5 m. The ladder is 2.5 m long.

🧩 Method
  • 1. Convert units to make them consistent.
  • 2. Identify the hypotenuse.
  • 3. Apply Pythagoras.
  • 4. Take the square root and give the answer with the correct unit.

Exercise 3 — Two methods for the same triangle

Right-angled triangle: vertical side = 20 cm, bottom-right angle = 35°, hypotenuse = x cm. Find x (3 s.f.) using two different methods.

📖 Identifying the sides
Opposite side
20 cm (facing the 35° angle).
Adjacent side
unknown, called y.
Hypotenuse
x (the longest side).

Method 1 — Sine ratio (Success)

We know the opposite and want the hypotenuse → use sine.

\(\sin(35°) = \dfrac{20}{x}\)

Isolate x:

\(x = \dfrac{20}{\sin(35°)} \approx 34.8689 \text{ cm}\)

To 3 s.f.: x ≈ 34.9 cm.

Method 2 — Tangent + Pythagoras (Mizgin)

We don't know the adjacent, so start with tangent to find it.

\(\tan(35°) = \dfrac{20}{y} \Rightarrow y = \dfrac{20}{\tan(35°)} \approx 28.563 \text{ cm}\)

Then apply Pythagoras with the two shorter sides (20 cm and 28.563 cm):

\(x^2 = 20^2 + 28.563^2 = 400 + 815.8427 = 1215.8427\)

\(x = \sqrt{1215.8427} \approx 34.8689 \text{ cm}\)

To 3 s.f.: x ≈ 34.9 cm.

🧩 Comparison
  • Both methods give the same result (x ≈ 34.9 cm) — they are algebraically equivalent.
  • The sine method is more direct: one calculation.
  • Tangent + Pythagoras goes through the adjacent: two steps, but useful when both shorter sides are needed.
  • Choose the method based on the information given in the problem.

Circle theorems

📖 Definitions
Tangent–radius theorem
The angle between a tangent and the radius at the point of contact is 90°. angle tangente-rayon = 90°
Angle at the centre
The angle subtended by an arc at the centre is twice the angle subtended at the circumference. angle au centre = 2 × angle au périmètre
Angle in a semicircle
An angle in a semicircle is a right angle (90°). angle dans un demi-cercle = 90°

1. Tangent ⊥ radius

2. Angle at centre = 2 × Angle at circumference

3. Angle in a semicircle = 90°

📐 Why is the angle in a semicircle 90°?

Let AB be a diameter of a circle with centre O, and C a point on the circle. Triangle AOC is isosceles (OA = OC = radius), so angles OÂC and OĈA are equal — call them α. Similarly, triangle BOC is isosceles (OB = OC = radius), so angles OB̂C and OĈB are equal — call them β. The angle sum in triangle ABC is α + (α + β) + β = 180°, so 2(α + β) = 180°, i.e. α + β = 90°. But the angle at C in triangle ABC is exactly α + β = 90°: it is a right angle.

✏️ Worked example — circle theorems

An arc AB subtends an angle of 50° at the circumference. What angle does the same arc subtend at the centre?

1. Theorem: angle at centre = 2 × angle at circumference.

2. Computation: angle at centre = 2 × 50° = 100°.

3. Application: if a tangent passes through A, the angle between the tangent and the radius OA is 90°. And if AB is a diameter, any angle ACB inscribed in the semicircle is 90°.

✏️ Worked example — circle

A circle has radius 7 cm. Find its circumference and area (use \(\pi \approx 3.14\)).

1. Circumference: \(C = 2\pi r = 2 \times 3.14 \times 7 \approx 43.96\) cm.

2. Area: \(A = \pi r^2 = 3.14 \times 49 \approx 153.86\) cm².

🧩 Summary
  • The interior angle sum of any quadrilateral is always 360°.
  • Diagonals have specific properties depending on the quadrilateral (equal, perpendicular, bisecting each other).
  • Circle formulae: \(C = 2\pi r\), \(A = \pi r^2\).
  • Three key theorems: tangent ⊥ radius, angle at centre = 2 × angle at circumference, angle in a semicircle = 90°.

09Angles Essential

Angles are measured in degrees (°). There are several names depending on their size, and useful angle sums in triangles and circles.

📖 Definitions

An angle less than 90°. angle aigu
Right angle
An angle exactly equal to 90°. angle droit
Obtuse angle
An angle between 90° and 180°. angle obtus

An angle of exactly 180°. angle plat

An angle greater than 180° and less than 360°. angle rentrant

Two angles that add up to 90°. angles complémentaires

Two angles that add up to 180°. angles supplémentaires

✏️ Worked example

Two angles of a triangle measure 48° and 63°. Find the third angle.

Sum of angles in a triangle = 180°, so the third angle = \(180 - (48 + 63) = 180 - 111 = 69°\).

🧩 Summary
  • Angles are named by size: acute, right, obtuse, straight, reflex, full turn.
  • The angle in a semi-circle is always 90° (Thales’ theorem).

Solving Algebraically

A linear equation is an equation where the unknown appears only to the first power. If there are two unknowns, you need two simultaneous equations to find their values.

11Linear Equations Essential

📖 Definitions
Linear equation
An equation of the form \(ax + b = c\) or \(ax + by = c\). équation linéaire
Unknown
The value we are trying to find (often \(x\) or \(y\)). inconnue
Solution
A value that makes the equation true. solution
General form: \(\;ax + by = c\)
✏️ Worked example 1 — one unknown

Solve \(3x + 5 = 17\).

1. Subtract 5 from both sides: \(3x = 12\).

2. Divide both sides by 3: \(x = 4\).

Check: \(3(4) + 5 = 12 + 5 = 17\) ✓.

✏️ Worked example 2 — two unknowns

We know \(y = 16\) and the linear equation is \(y = 5x + 1\). Find \(x\).

1. Substitute \(y = 16\): \(16 = 5x + 1\).

2. Subtract 1: \(15 = 5x\).

3. Divide by 5: \(x = 3\).

Inequalities

There are four inequality signs:

SignMeaningFrench
\(x \le y\)\(x\) is less than or equal to \(y\)inférieur ou égal
\(x < y\)\(x\) is less than \(y\)strictement inférieur
\(x > y\)\(x\) is greater than \(y\)strictement supérieur
\(x \ge y\)\(x\) is greater than or equal to \(y\)supérieur ou égal
⚠ Common mistake

When you multiply or divide both sides of an inequality by a negative number, the direction of the inequality sign must be reversed. Example: \(-2x > 8 \Rightarrow x < -4\) (not \(x > -4\)).

✏️ Worked example — inequality

Solve \(2x - 6 > 2\).

1. Add 6: \(2x > 8\).

2. Divide by 2: \(x > 4\).

Now solve \(-2x - 6 > 2\): add 6 → \(-2x > 8\); divide by −2 (reverse the sign) → \(x < -4\).

🧩 Summary
  • Isolate the unknown by doing the same operation on both sides.
  • When multiplying or dividing by a negative number, reverse the inequality sign.

12Simultaneous Equations Essential

If there are two unknowns, you need two simultaneous equations. They can be solved graphically (the intersection point of the two lines) or algebraically (by substitution or by elimination).

Example system: \(\begin{cases} 2x - y = 3 \\ x + 2y = 14 \end{cases}\)

Graphical method

Plot both lines. The coordinates of their point of intersection give the values of the unknowns. In the example above, the lines intersect at \(x = 4\) and \(y = 5\).

Method 1 — Substitution

Isolate one unknown

From the second equation: \(x = -2y + 14\).

Substitute

Into the first equation: \(2(-2y + 14) - y = 3\).

Solve for \(y\)

\(-4y + 28 - y = 3 \Rightarrow -5y = -25 \Rightarrow y = 5\).

Find \(x\)

\(x = -2(5) + 14 = 4\).

Conclusion

Solution: \(x = 4\), \(y = 5\).

Method 2 — Elimination

Multiply to match coefficients

Multiply the first equation by 2: \(4x - 2y = 6\).

Add or subtract

Add to the second equation: \(4x - 2y + x + 2y = 6 + 14 \Rightarrow 5x = 20\).

Solve for \(x\)

\(x = 4\).

Substitute back

\(4 + 2y = 14 \Rightarrow 2y = 10 \Rightarrow y = 5\).

✏️ Worked example — dinosaur figurines

Anna has triceratops (3 horns, 4 legs) and allosaurus (2 horns, 2 legs) figurines. Her brother counts 73 horns and 86 legs. How many of each does she have?

1. Let \(x\) = triceratops, \(y\) = allosaurus.

2. Horns: \(3x + 2y = 73\).

3. Legs: \(4x + 2y = 86\).

4. Subtract: \((4x + 2y) - (3x + 2y) = 86 - 73 \Rightarrow x = 13\).

5. Substitute: \(3(13) + 2y = 73 \Rightarrow 39 + 2y = 73 \Rightarrow 2y = 34 \Rightarrow y = 17\).

Conclusion: Anna has 13 triceratops and 17 allosaurus figurines.

Check: horns \(3(13)+2(17) = 39+34 = 73\) ✓; legs \(4(13)+2(17) = 52+34 = 86\) ✓.

🧩 Summary
  • Two unknowns → two equations.
  • Substitution: isolate one unknown and replace it in the other equation.
  • Elimination: make the coefficients of one unknown match, then add or subtract.
  • Graphically, the solution is the intersection point of the two lines.

Domain 1 — Mapping

Functions: domain, range, graph

A function is a rule that assigns exactly one output to each input. The domain is the set of allowed inputs; the range is the set of possible outputs.

14Definition of a Function Essential

📖 Definitions
Function
A rule that maps each input to exactly one output. fonction
Domain
The set of all allowed input values. ensemble de définition
Range
The set of all output values the function can produce. ensemble image
Image
The output of a function for a specific input. image
Preimage
The input(s) that produce a given output. antécédent
Monotonic
A function that is either always increasing or always decreasing. monotone
Notation: \(\;f : x \mapsto f(x)\)    Domain: \(D_f\)    Range: \(R_f\)
✏️ Worked example

For \(f(x) = x^2 - 1\), find the image of 3 and the preimages of 8.

1. Image of 3: \(f(3) = 3^2 - 1 = 8\).

2. Preimages of 8: solve \(x^2 - 1 = 8 \Rightarrow x^2 = 9 \Rightarrow x = 3\) or \(x = -3\).

🧩 Summary
  • Every input has exactly one output (vertical line test).
  • The domain can be restricted (e.g. denominator ≠ 0, square root ≥ 0).

15Linear & Reciprocal Functions Essential

Linear functions

\(f(x) = mx + c\)    \(m\) = gradient (slope), \(c\) = \(y\)-intercept.
✏️ Worked example

For \(f(x) = 2x + 4\), find \(f(3)\) and the \(x\)-intercept.

1. \(f(3) = 2(3) + 4 = 10\).

2. \(x\)-intercept: \(2x + 4 = 0 \Rightarrow x = -2\).

Reciprocal function

\(f(x) = \dfrac{1}{x}\)    Domain: \(x \ne 0\)    Range: \(y \ne 0\)

The graph is a hyperbola with two branches. The axes are asymptotes: the curve gets closer to them but never touches them.

🧩 Summary
  • Linear functions have a constant gradient; their graph is a straight line.
  • The reciprocal function is undefined at \(x = 0\), and its graph has two asymptotes.

16Quadratic Functions Essential

A quadratic function is of the form \(f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c\) with \(a \ne 0\). Its graph is a parabola.

📖 Definitions
Quadratic function
Function of the form \(ax^2 + bx + c\). fonction du second degré
Discriminant
\(\Delta = b^2 - 4ac\); determines the number of real roots. discriminant
Root (zero)
A value of \(x\) for which \(f(x) = 0\). racine
Completing the square
Rewriting \(ax^2+bx+c\) as \(a(x-\alpha)^2+\beta\). mise sous forme canonique
\(\Delta = b^2 - 4ac\)    Roots: \(x = \dfrac{-b \pm \sqrt{\Delta}}{2a}\)
DiscriminantNumber of real roots
\(\Delta > 0\)Two distinct real roots
\(\Delta = 0\)One repeated root \(x = -b/(2a)\)
\(\Delta < 0\)No real roots
📐 Proof — the quadratic formula from completing the square

Start with \(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\). Divide by \(a\): \(x^2 + \frac{b}{a}x + \frac{c}{a} = 0\). Complete the square: \(\left(x + \frac{b}{2a}\right)^2 - \frac{b^2}{4a^2} + \frac{c}{a} = 0\), so \(\left(x + \frac{b}{2a}\right)^2 = \frac{b^2 - 4ac}{4a^2} = \frac{\Delta}{4a^2}\). Taking square roots: \(x + \frac{b}{2a} = \pm \frac{\sqrt{\Delta}}{2a}\), hence \(x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{\Delta}}{2a}\).

✏️ Worked example

Solve \(2x^2 - 4x - 6 = 0\).

1. Discriminant: \(\Delta = (-4)^2 - 4(2)(-6) = 16 + 48 = 64 = 8^2\).

2. Roots: \(x = \dfrac{4 \pm 8}{4}\), so \(x_1 = -1\) and \(x_2 = 3\).

3. Check: \(x_1 + x_2 = 2 = -b/a\) ✓, \(x_1 x_2 = -3 = c/a\) ✓.

4. Sign: since \(a = 2 > 0\), \(f(x) > 0\) outside the roots, \(f(x) < 0\) between them.

🧩 Summary
  • The sign of \(\Delta\) tells you how many real roots there are.
  • Completing the square gives both the vertex \((\alpha,\beta)\) and the quadratic formula.
  • Relations: \(x_1 + x_2 = -b/a\) and \(x_1 x_2 = c/a\).

18Discrete Data Essential

Discrete data takes only specific, separate values (e.g. number of siblings). We use measures of central tendency (mean, median, mode) and spread (range, interquartile range), and can draw a box plot.

📖 Definitions
Mean
Sum of values ÷ number of values. moyenne
Median
Middle value when data is ordered. médiane
Mode
Most frequent value. mode
Range
Maximum − minimum. étendue
Interquartile range (IQR)
\(Q_3 - Q_1\). écart interquartile
Box plot
A diagram showing minimum, Q1, median, Q3, maximum. boîte à moustaches
✏️ Worked example

Data: 3, 5, 5, 7, 8, 9, 11. Find the mean, median and mode.

1. Mean: \((3+5+5+7+8+9+11)/7 = 48/7 \approx 6.86\).

2. Median: the middle value (4th) is 7.

3. Mode: 5 appears twice, so mode = 5.

🧩 Summary
  • Mean is sensitive to outliers; median is more robust.
  • IQR measures the spread of the middle 50% of the data.

19Continuous Data Recommended

Continuous data can take any value in an interval (e.g. height, time). We group it into class intervals and use histograms or cumulative frequency graphs.

Estimated mean from grouped data: \(\bar{x} \approx \dfrac{\sum f_i \times m_i}{\sum f_i}\) where \(m_i\) is the midpoint of each class.
🧩 Summary
  • Continuous data is grouped into intervals; the midpoint represents each interval.
  • Box plots work for both discrete and continuous data.
Domain 5 — Advanced Geometry

Angles & Bearings

Three-figure bearings are used to describe direction. Angles formed by parallel lines have special names that let you find unknown angles.

20Bearings & Angle Vocabulary Essential

📖 Definitions
Three-figure bearing
An angle measured clockwise from North, written with three digits (e.g. 045°). relèvement / azimut
Complementary angles
Two angles adding up to 90°. angles complémentaires
Supplementary angles
Two angles adding up to 180°. angles supplémentaires
Vertically opposite angles
Angles opposite each other when two lines cross; they are equal. angles opposés par le sommet
Alternate angles
Angles on opposite sides of a transversal between parallel lines; they are equal. angles alternes-internes
Corresponding angles
Angles in matching positions on parallel lines; they are equal. angles correspondants
Bearings are always written with 3 digits: 045°, 120°, 270°.
🧩 Summary
  • Bearings: clockwise from North, three figures.
  • Parallel line rules: alternate angles are equal, corresponding angles are equal, co-interior angles add to 180°.

21Coordinate Geometry Essential

Coordinate geometry links algebra and geometry: points have coordinates, lines have equations, and distances and gradients can be calculated from coordinates.

📖 Definitions
Coordinates
A pair \((x, y)\) locating a point. coordonnées
Midpoint
The point halfway between two points. milieu
Gradient
The slope of a line, \(m = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}\). coefficient directeur / pente
Distance: \(\;d = \sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2 + (y_2-y_1)^2}\)    Midpoint: \(\;\left(\dfrac{x_1+x_2}{2},\dfrac{y_1+y_2}{2}\right)\)
Gradient: \(\;m = \dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}\)    Equation of a line: \(\;y = mx + c\)
✏️ Worked example

Given \(A(2, 3)\) and \(B(6, 11)\), find the length, midpoint and gradient of \([AB]\), then the equation of the line through \(A\) and \(B\).

1. Length: \(d = \sqrt{(6-2)^2+(11-3)^2} = \sqrt{16+64} = \sqrt{80} = 4\sqrt5\).

2. Midpoint: \(\left(\frac{2+6}{2},\frac{3+11}{2}\right) = (4, 7)\).

3. Gradient: \(m = \frac{11-3}{6-2} = \frac{8}{4} = 2\).

4. Equation: \(y = 2x + c\). Using \(A(2,3)\): \(3 = 4 + c \Rightarrow c = -1\). So \(y = 2x - 1\).

⚠ Common mistake

Do not subtract the coordinates in the wrong order — the formula \(\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}\) is consistent as long as you keep \(A\) and \(B\) in the same order for both numerator and denominator.

🧩 Summary
  • Distance, midpoint and gradient are all derived from coordinates.
  • The equation of a line needs either two points or one point and the gradient.
  • Parallel lines have the same gradient; perpendicular lines have gradients whose product is −1.
